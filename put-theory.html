English verb in focus

PUTin Swedish

The English verb PUT is extremely general. Swedish often specifies an object’s position, attachment, movement into a container, clothing or expression in words by choosing a different verb.

With PUT, ask where the object ends up and how it is positioned or attached.

Why PUT is difficult in Swedish

English can use put regardless of whether an object lies flat, stands upright, is attached to a surface, goes into a container or is worn.

Swedish often builds this information into the verb itself. The form, position and destination of the object therefore matter.

What does PUT mean here?

Will the object lie flat? → lägga
Will the object stand upright? → ställa
Is the object attached to a surface? → sätta
Is somebody dressing? → ta på sig
Is something being put inside? → stoppa
Is an idea being expressed in words? → formulera

Six Swedish ways to translate PUT

Each choice below expresses a different type of event. The English examples use ordinary British English, followed by the natural Swedish equivalent.

lägga
lay · put horizontally

Use lägga when the object is placed flat, horizontally or in a lying position.

Put the book on the table.
Lägg boken på bordet.
She put the keys beside the phone.
Hon lade nycklarna bredvid telefonen.
Where did you put the newspaper?
Var lade du tidningen?
The result of lägga is normally expressed with ligga: Jag lägger boken där – boken ligger där.
ställa
stand · put upright

Use ställa when the object is placed upright or in a standing position.

Put the vase on the shelf.
Ställ vasen på hyllan.
He put the bottle in the fridge.
Han ställde flaskan i kylskåpet.
Where shall I put the chair?
Var ska jag ställa stolen?
The result of ställa is normally expressed with stå: Jag ställer flaskan där – flaskan står där.
sätta
attach · put in position

Use sätta when something is attached, fixed or placed in a position not described as lying or standing.

Put the poster on the wall.
Sätt affischen på väggen.
She put a label on the box.
Hon satte en etikett på lådan.
Where did you put the stamp?
Var satte du frimärket?
For a framed picture, Swedish often uses the more specific verb hänga: hänga tavlan på väggen.
ta på sig
put on · dress

Use ta på sig when put on refers to clothes, shoes or accessories.

Put on your coat.
Ta på dig kappan.
He put on his shoes.
Han tog på sig skorna.
I need to put on something warmer.
Jag måste ta på mig något varmare.
Swedish ta på sig is reflexive: jag tar på mig, du tar på dig, hon tar på sig.
stoppa
put into · insert

Use stoppa when put means inserting something into a bag, pocket, box or other enclosed space.

Put it in your bag.
Stoppa det i väskan.
She put the letter in her pocket.
Hon stoppade brevet i fickan.
He put the money into an envelope.
Han stoppade pengarna i ett kuvert.
Swedish lägga is also possible in many of these situations. Stoppa emphasises movement into an enclosed space.
formulera
express · put into words

Use formulera when put means expressing a thought, feeling or argument in words.

It is difficult to put it into words.
Det är svårt att formulera det.
She put her thoughts into words.
Hon formulerade sina tankar.
How would you put that in Swedish?
Hur skulle du formulera det på svenska?
Here put is abstract: nothing is physically moved. Swedish uses the specific communication verb formulera.

How PUT divides into six meanings

Use the meaning of the complete expression — not the English verb by itself — to choose the Swedish equivalent.

Swedish choiceCore meaningTypical English PUT patternDiagnostic question
lägga placing something flat or horizontal Put the book on the table. Will the object lie flat?
ställa placing something upright Put the vase on the shelf. Will the object stand upright?
sätta attaching or fixing something Put the poster on the wall. Is the object attached to a surface?
ta på sig putting clothes on the body Put on your coat. Is somebody dressing?
stoppa putting something into a container Put it in your bag. Is something being put inside?
formulera expressing an idea verbally It is difficult to put it into words. Is an idea being expressed in words?
Important: English also has more specific verbs and expressions. The difficulty is that everyday English allows put in all six situations, whereas Swedish normally makes the semantic category more explicit.
