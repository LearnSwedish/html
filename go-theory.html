GOin Swedish

The English verb GO can describe movement, travel, a change of state, compatibility, a mechanism functioning or something disappearing and being lost. Swedish normally chooses a verb that specifies which kind of event is involved.

GO may describe movement, change, functioning or loss. Identify the event before choosing the Swedish verb.

Why GO is difficult in Swedish

English go is not limited to physical movement. Things can go bad, colours can go together, clocks can refuse to go, and opportunities can simply go.

Swedish normally separates these meanings and uses a distinct verb for each one.

gå åka bli passa fungera försvinna

What does GO mean here?

Is somebody moving on foot? → gå
Is transport involved? → åka
Does something change state? → bli
Do two things match? → passa
Does a clock or mechanism work? → fungera
Is something lost or disappearing? → försvinna

Six Swedish ways to translate GO

Each choice below expresses a different type of event. The English examples use ordinary British English, followed by the natural Swedish equivalent.

gå
walk · go on foot

Use gå when go means moving on foot or leaving on foot.

I go home at five.
Jag går hem klockan fem.
Shall we go to the park?
Ska vi gå till parken?
He went straight to the station.
Han gick direkt till stationen.
If transport is involved, Swedish normally uses åka instead. Context determines whether English go home is gå hem or åka hem.
åka
travel · go by transport

Use åka when go describes travelling by vehicle or public transport.

I go by bus every day.
Jag åker buss varje dag.
We are going to Gothenburg tomorrow.
Vi åker till Göteborg i morgon.
They went home by taxi.
De åkte taxi hem.
Swedish distinguishes movement on foot, gå, from travel by transport, åka, more consistently than English does.
bli
become · change state

Use bli when go + adjective describes a change from one state to another.

Milk goes bad quickly in the heat.
Mjölk blir snabbt dålig i värmen.
His hair is going grey.
Hans hår börjar bli grått.
Something went wrong.
Något blev fel.
The pattern go + adjective often corresponds to Swedish bli + adjective.
passa
match · go with

Use passa when go with means matching, combining well or looking suitable together.

This tie goes with your shirt.
Den här slipsen passar till din skjorta.
Blue goes well with grey.
Blått passar bra ihop med grått.
Do these shoes go with the dress?
Passar de här skorna till klänningen?
Use passa till or passa ihop when the issue is visual or practical compatibility.
fungera
work · run · go

Use fungera (or gå) when a clock, mechanism or system operates or works properly.

The clock won't go.
Klockan vill inte fungera.
How is your new job going?
Hur fungerar ditt nya jobb?
The machine stopped going.
Maskinen slutade fungera.
In everyday Swedish, simple gå is also common for machinery and clocks (klockan går), while fungera highlights the operational aspect.
försvinna
disappear · go · be lost

Use försvinna (or expressions like gå förlorad / ryka) when something vanishes, is lost, or has to go.

There goes my last chance.
Där försvann min sista chans.
That sentence had better go altogether.
Den meningen bör försvinna helt och hållet.
All my money went in a day.
Alla mina pengar försvann på en dag.
Depending on context, English go in the sense of elimination or loss can also be rendered as bort (måste bort) or ryka in colloquial speech.

How GO divides into six meanings

Use the meaning of the complete expression — not the English verb by itself — to choose the Swedish equivalent.

Swedish choice Core meaning Typical English GO pattern Diagnostic question
gå movement on foot I go home at five. Is somebody moving on foot?
åka movement using transport I go by bus every day. Is transport involved?
bli changing into an unwanted state Milk goes bad quickly in the heat. Does something change state?
passa matching or looking suitable together This tie goes with your shirt. Do two things match?
fungera a clock or mechanism operating The clock won't go. Does a clock or mechanism work?
försvinna something vanishing or being lost There goes my last chance. Is something lost or disappearing?
Important: English also has more specific verbs and expressions. The difficulty is that everyday English allows go in all six situations, whereas Swedish normally makes the semantic category more explicit.
